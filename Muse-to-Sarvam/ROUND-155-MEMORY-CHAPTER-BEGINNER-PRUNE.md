### "A `kosh` grows" — and a slice taken before the growth

**TRUE, and the reason is beginner-followable.** §5: "When a `kosh` grows, its elements move to a larger place, and a slice kept from before would still look at the old one. So the compiler refuses, before the program runs, every way a slice could be kept past a growth." Three refusals are shown, each with its code: store in a name (`E_KOSH_VIBHAG`), slice to a `prakriya` that can grow the `kosh` (`E_KOSH_VIBHAG`), slice used as a number (`E_VIBHAG_ANKA`). Both codes are verified at their emission sites: `E_KOSH_VIBHAG` headers at `src/sutram_compiler.asm:628/:649/:652`, emitted by `r129_check_call` (`:28909`), `r134_kosh_slice` (`:28300`), the FFI door (`src/r32_ffi.inc:699`, `call r134_holds_kosh`), and the walk's doors; `E_VIBHAG_ANKA` header at `:631`, emitted by the walk's `.num` handler (`:28692`, inside `r137_check_stmt`, `:28454`). All refusals are compile-time — "before the program runs" is exact.

**The completeness caveat:** "every way" is true of the compiler (all fourteen shapes refused at compile time, plus the FFI shape closed at v314 — the foreign-funcall path now calls `r134_holds_kosh`, `src/r32_ffi.inc:690-710`; the indirect-call shape is refused by `r129_check_call`'s `.unknown` path, `:28968-28980`), but the chapter shows three ways and does not mention the FFI shape, the indirect-call shape, or the other shapes (mala-cell, global, rachana field, raw memory, dasham parameter, untyped return, two-function chain, module initializer, comparison-as-index, inline asm). A reader cannot check "every way" from the chapter alone. The rule statement itself ("use a slice where you make it, pass it straight to a `prakriya` that reads it, do not keep it") is accurate guidance. The descriptor is never named — but the chapter does not need the word to state the reason, and the reason it states (elements move; a kept slice would look at the old place) is the true one.

### "Everything made inside a block is gone when you leave" — is that true of a `kosh`?

**The chapter does not say that, and it is right not to.** §2 scopes the giving-back to `nirmmita` ("everything made with `nirmmita` inside the block is given back"), §3 says "A `kosh` keeps its bytes for the whole program, even one made inside a block, so its share of `parimana()` never falls", and §7 says "a `kosh` made inside a block cannot leave it (`E_KOSH_MANDAPA`)". All three hold, with a mechanism correction:

- `E_KOSH_MANDAPA` **exists** — 7 occurrences in `src/sutram_compiler.asm`. Compile-time doors `r107_msg_var/mem/ret` (`:557/:559/:561`, via `r73_fail_*` at `:12354-12366`) and `r108_msg_keep` (`:569`, via `r108_check_call` at `:12764-12811`), plus the runtime generation check (`r82_m1` at `:438`, prakriya-entry param check per the R82 header comment at `:10642-10656`).
- But a `kosh` made inside a `mandapa` does **not** live in the scratch cursor: `r108_kosh_alloc` (`:12655-12677`) only remembers the kosh and jumps to `r101a_emit_kosh_alloc` — "the storage is unchanged … No arena slot: reclaiming waits for the descriptor" (`:12653-12654`); "The kosh's storage is on the persistent side" (`:10642-10656`). What ends at block end is its **liveness**, not its storage. That is why §3's "keeps its bytes for the whole program, even one made inside a block" is consistent with the code.
- Two nuances the chapter does not state: (a) a **read-only** pass to a `prakriya` that does not keep the `kosh` **is** allowed — `r108_check_call` refuses only may-keep parameters and unknown callees (`:12764-12790`); "cannot leave it" means cannot be *kept* outside. (b) The runtime check fires only when the `kosh` is passed as a parameter to a `prakriya`; direct use of the name after the block in the same function is outside its scope — the block's scope closes at `r7_close_block` (`:8397`).

### The measured numbers: 52,760 KB and 13,480 KB

**Those are not the chapter's numbers — they are the v284 numbers, and the chapter is right not to use them.** The chapter gives **392 KB with the block, 49,920 KB without** (Linux, v315, "the largest resident memory of the whole run"), and attributes them as a measurement: "That is one machine and one program, not a promise for every program." Sarvam's 52,760 / 13,480 KB are from ROUND-99, measured at v284 (Claude's R157 hand-back: "Your number is v284's; v315 measures 392 KB against 49,920 KB. I ran the same program on v315. `/usr/bin/time -v` gives the largest resident memory: 49,920 KB without the block, 392 KB with it. The block's cost fell from 13.5 MB to 0.4 MB since v284."). The R102-103 hand-back says the same: "your 52,760 KB without the block is real (49,920 KB here)".

**Are the chapter's numbers the numbers the code produces? Could not determine from source** — verifying an RSS peak needs running, which the brief forbids. What reading can say: 200,000 × 256 = 51,200,000 bytes = 50,000 KiB. Without the block, every buffer stays live (bump only, never reused — `src/r65_arena.inc:23`) and every page is touched (`b[0] = i`, 16 buffers per page), so peak RSS ≈ 51.2 MB; 49,920 KB is within 0.16% of the naive 50,000 KiB — plausible, the 80 KiB shortfall not explained by the code. With the block, the same page(s) are reused every call (rewind at block end), so RSS ≈ program baseline; 392 KB is plausible. The loop count (`tak` inclusive → 200,000 calls) and the sum (1+…+200000 = 20,000,100,000) check out. The reference's adjacent measurement is the same shape at a different scale (`docs/LANGUAGE-REFERENCE.md:1290-1293`: 1,000,000 × `nirmmita(64)`: 264 KB with the block, 62 MB without). **Why the block's cost fell from 13,480 KB (v284) to 392 KB (v315): could not determine from source** — that needs the v284 tree and its scratch implementation. The chapter's attribution ("one machine and one program, not a promise") is the right one, and it is the sentence the numbers need.

### `parimana()` — what does it count?

**TRUE on the numbers; the chapter's one-sentence answer is accurate but incomplete.** §3: "It counts what the program asked for, not what the operating system reports." The implementation (`.ge_call_parimana`, `src/sutram_compiler.asm:23892-23951`) sums **(cur − base) + (sima − end) of the arena + the kosh regions' counter (`0parimana`)** — logical bytes **rounded up to a multiple of 16**, not reserved and not committed. The arena is 1 GiB *reserved* (`R65_ARENA equ 0x40000000`, `src/r65_arena.inc:54`) but reservation is not counted; no OS-reported figure (RSS, committed pages) is consulted. The computation is the same emitted code on both targets ("ELF and PE (R123)", `:23895`); since v300 the Windows commit behaviour matches Linux (`docs/LANGUAGE-REFERENCE.md:1299`). So "logical bytes" is the answer to the brief's three-way question — neither reserved nor committed — and it holds on both platforms.

**What the chapter does not say, and a reader needs:** `parimana()` counts kosh **capacity**, not length. `0parimana` tracks current capacity × 8 (`src/r65_arena.inc:612/672/708`). Verified against the chapter's numbers:

- `nirmmita(1000)` inside a block → 1008 (align16(1000)). ✓
- `parimana()` = 0 before anything, and 0 after the block ends (the rewind restores the scratch cursor). ✓
- The "putting it together" example, `parimana()` = **96**: the `kosh vargah[4]` is made outside any block; creation allocates (4+4)×8 = 64 bytes; the first four pushes fit (capacity 4); the fifth grows 4→8 (`newcap = cap ? cap*2 : 4`, `:22500-22510`), in place, new cursor = align16(32 + 64) = **96**. So 96 = 32 (header) + 8×8 (**capacity 8**). Had it counted length (5), it would be 32 + 40 = 72. The chapter says "96: only the kosh — every block gave its 16 bytes back" without the breakdown. Corroborated by the tree's own examples: `examples/317` ("a kosh of 1000 8288" = 64 + 8224) and `examples/318` ("grown to capacity 4 96", "a block's nirmmita(100) 208", "after the block 224").

**One flagged reading (could not determine from source).** Decoding `r107_p1` (`:564`: `cmp rax,0x1000; jb +47; push rax`) against the emitted sequence, the `jb` target lands at the **second byte of `r107_p3`'s `jmp +2`** — one byte short of the `xor eax,eax` that produces 0 — i.e. on a path that would decode garbage including a write to `[rax+0x48]` with rax=0. **But** the suite passes 327 including `examples/317`, whose *first* statement calls `parimana()` before any allocation and expects `start 0` — so either the arena is already reserved at that point (not established from source), or the path is never taken, or the byte count is off by one somewhere. The byte decode and the passing test evidence conflict, and reading alone cannot resolve which is wrong. Needs execution (a minimal program whose first act is `parimana()`, or a disassembly of a compiled binary).

### The out-of-memory message — quoted exactly? And is the `kosh` unchanged?

**Yes to both, and the second half is stated as the chapter's own emphasis.** §6 quotes:

`Sutram truti: kosh vardhayitum smritih na labdha - kosh yatha asit tatha eva`

That string exists **verbatim** at `src/sutram_compiler.asm:708` (`r127_msg_grow`). The chapter then says: "The second half is the thing to know: the kosh was not damaged. It still holds exactly what it held before the push that failed, so the program never leaves a half-written kosh behind. The program still stops, though. That is not a failure you can catch and carry on from."

**Verified from the code ordering.** `r127_emit_grow_check` (`src/sutram_compiler.asm:21380-21420`) fires when rax=0 after the allocation, prints via `likha`, exits 1. In the grow path (`src/r65_arena.inc:562-720`), the check is called at `:596` and `:682`, **before** the copy loop (`:690-700`) and **before** the descriptor update (`:718-720`); the old region is munmap'd (`:713`) only *after* the copy completes. On failure nothing is written, copied, or freed — the elements pointer, length, and capacity are exactly as they were. Exit status is 1 by construction on both platforms (`mov eax,60; mov edi,1; syscall` on Linux; `emit_syscall`'s PE shim on Windows). **Whether the R137 crash-after-message (0xC00000FD) applies to *this* message on Windows: could not determine from source** — R137 demonstrated it only for the `rt_veh` handler path. The mechanism (ExitProcess teardown under an exhausted commit limit) is the same shape, so it is plausible but unproven for the grow path. On Linux the exit is a plain syscall with no teardown, so no such crash. (The creation-time analogue, `r127_msg_make` at `:709` — `kosh rachayitum smritih na labdha` — is not quoted in the chapter; the chapter covers only the growth case.)

### The honest paragraph on what the language does not have

**All four named, all four true, and the chapter avoids implying any of them exist.** §7: "no garbage collector (nothing runs in the background looking for memory to reclaim); no free for you to call (memory comes back when a block ends, or when the program ends — you never call anything to give memory back); no reuse inside a live block (a block gives its memory back only when it ends, all at once); no threads (one program does one thing at a time)."

- **No reuse inside a live block — TRUE.** The scratch cursor `[end]` moves only down on allocation and is restored only by a rewind at block end / return / break; no other store to it exists.
- **No free — TRUE.** Grep for a free builtin name in `src/` and `lib/` (`"free"`, `bn_free`, `bn_mukt`): 0 hits; no `BN_FREE`/`BN_MUKT` in the builtin table. (`tyaj` at `:814` is the REPL exit command, not a free. The runtime *does* munmap/VirtualFree old kosh regions internally — `src/r65_arena.inc:713`, `win/winrt_blob.asm:184-188,312` — but that is the runtime's business, not a user-callable free; the hand-back's own qualification.)
- **No threads — TRUE.** No thread builtin; the only "thread" hits are PE startup OS-thread setup (`:2694/2707`) and a metaphor in `lib/phal.smlib:13` ("threading a separate ok cell through every call" = passing a value through calls).
- **No GC — TRUE.** Grep for `garbage|collector|mark-sweep` in `src/`, `lib/`, `win/*.asm`: 0 relevant hits.

**One clarity caveat in the same paragraph.** The closing sentence says "a `kosh` that cannot grow, a block over its budget, or **a block field that is full** each stops the program with a sentence that says which, not a crash." All three are real refusals — `r127_msg_grow` (`:708`), `E_PARIMITA` (`:8916`), and `kshetra purnam` (`src/sutram_compiler.asm:728`, emitted by the scratch `.full` path, `src/r65_arena.inc:454-460`). But the chapter never defines **"field"**: it is the reference's word for the kshetra, the 1 GiB arena (`docs/LANGUAGE-REFERENCE.md:1296-1297`: "More than the field holds … `Sutram truti: kshetra purnam - mandapa-smritih samapta` (the field is full — the block's memory is used up)"), while the word "field" in the compiler source almost entirely means *rachana* fields. A reader meeting "a block field that is full" in chapter 19 must infer it means the kshetra. Grounded, not wrong; compressed.

### The other claims, checked

- **§1 "A `mala` has the size you give it, for ever" — TRUE.** A mala's allocation is fixed at declaration; no growth path exists for a mala anywhere — growth is kosh-only (`r127_emit_grow`, `src/r65_arena.inc:577`).
- **§1 "A `kosh` starts small and grows each time you add to it with `kosh_kship`" — FALSE as written.** Growth happens **only when the kosh is full**: `kosh_kship`'s codegen does `cmp r9,r10; jb .space` (`src/sutram_compiler.asm:22490-22497` — if length < capacity, straight to the append path), and only otherwise computes `newcap = cap ? cap*2 : 4` (`:22500-22510`) and calls `r127_emit_grow`. In the chapter's own §1 example (`kosh ankah[2]`, three pushes), only the **third** push grows (2→4); the first two do not. The sentence should say "grows when it is full". **This is the chapter's one false sentence.**
- **§1 "Reading `dinah[3]` stops the program with `Sutram truti: mala ank seema atikram`" — TRUE.** Message verbatim at `src/sutram_compiler.asm:385`; the check `emit_array_bounds_check` (`:21130-21160`) is emitted on the index path in both statement (`:19845`) and expression (`:21942`) codegen, so a **read** is checked, not just a write.
- **§2 "`nirmmita(n)` gives you n bytes of fresh, zeroed memory" — TRUE.** Scratch: `r73_gen_scratch_nirm` (`src/r65_arena.inc:420-445`) zeroes `ceil((size+7)/8)` qwords at **every** allocation ("zero it (memory is reused)", `:441`), so on the second entry into a block in a loop the reused addresses are zeroed afresh. Persistent: the arena is mmap'd and never reused, so pages arrive zeroed (`:23`). Caveat within the claim: only the requested n bytes are zeroed; alignment padding beyond them may not be.
- **§2 "however it ends (its last line, `nivartanam`, `krama`)" — TRUE for all three named endings.** Fall off: the rewind node built at `:8382-8395` → `r73_gen_rewind` sets `[end]` = the mark (`src/r65_arena.inc:404-412`). `nivartanam`: `r73_ret_mark` (`:8420-8433`) attaches the outermost mark; `.gs_return` calls `r73_gen_ret_rewind` (`:535-544`) for valued and valueless returns (also failure returns, `:17195`). `krama`/`agrim`: `r73_wrap_jump` (`:8437-8463`, call sites `:15609/15622`). A failure *inside* the block that stops the program exits outright — no rewind needed, none claimed.
- **§4 `E_PARIMITA` — TRUE, with the line's referent clarified.** Message format exact (`r103_msg_a/b/c`, `:8916-8918`). "pankti 2" is correct — N is the line of the **`mandapa` keyword** (`r80_line_of` of `r82_tmp`, set in `r72_parse_mandapa` at `:8338-8340`, printed at `:9043-9046`), not the offending allocation's line (line 5 of the example). The chapter prints "pankti 2" without saying it is the block's line — correct number, unexplained referent. Refusal is **runtime** (emitted code in `r103_emit_budget_checks`, `:9009-9073`, called from `r73_gen_scratch_nirm`), after `512` prints, exit 1. Budget covers (a) `nirmmita` inside the block — yes, including nested budgets (`:8909-8910`); (b) a kosh's growth — **no** (`r127_emit_grow` never calls the budget check; header comment `:8910-8912`); (c) a called `prakriya`'s allocations — **no**, same comment. Platform note: the R103 header comment says "Linux ELF only, as the block (PE: inert, unchecked)" (`:8912`), but that looks **stale** — no `target_pe` gate exists in the budget/scratch paths, and the PE runtime implements the arena (`win/winrt_blob.asm:169,179,293`). Whether the refusal *behaves* identically on native Windows is **could not determine from source** (no native run has verified it).
- **§5 "a whole `kosh` is passed as `k[:]`" (`likha(yoga(k))` = 10) — consistent with the tree.** Claude ran the chapter's examples on v315 (R157 hand-back: nine complete programs in each book; six run to the exit the book states, three are refused with the code the book states; the reader example prints 5). The whole-kosh-as-slice behaviour is the documented §17 rule.

## Task 2 — could a person who has never programmed read this chapter and write a program that uses a block, a budget and a `kosh`?

**No, not from this chapter alone — and that is the wrong test for chapter 19 of a book.** Read as a stranger, the chapter uses `vitti`, `chakra … tak`, `prakriya`, `nivartanam`, `likha`, `kosh_kship`, `kosh_gan`, `pankti_gan` without explaining any of them. A never-programmed reader cannot write the "putting it together" program from this chapter alone; they would not know what a `prakriya` is, what `tak` includes, or how a program is run at all (the chapter never says how to compile or run anything). With chapters 2–8 first, it is enough for the three things — the chapter gives all three in one runnable example and sets a budget to find by experiment — with four gaps:

1. **The chapter's own numbers are not derivable by the reader.** Nothing in it teaches the kosh growth policy (doubles when full) or that `parimana()` counts **capacity**, not length. The final example's 96 is therefore a magic number: a beginner who counts 5 numbers × 8 bytes = 40, plus a header they were never told is 32 bytes, gets 72, not 96, and has no way to find the missing 24. This is the essential missing piece for the chapter itself — it is the one gap that stops a reader from checking their own understanding against the chapter's own example.
2. **The budget does not count the `kosh`, and the combined example silently relies on it.** §4 states the rule ("The budget covers the `nirmmita` written inside the block, not a `kosh`"), but the "putting it together" example puts `kosh_kship(vargah, t[0])` **inside** a `mandapa parimita 256` without calling attention to it. A beginner combining the two will wonder whether the kosh's growth counts against the 256 — it does not — and the chapter never says so where the example needs it.
3. **"A block field that is full" is undefined** (see Task 1) — a first-time reader has to guess that "field" means the kshetra, while the only other sense of "field" they will meet is a rachana field.
4. **The out-of-memory message cannot be seen happening.** The chapter shows the message but gives the reader no way to make it happen; a program that exhausts memory is not one to put in a beginner's book, and `--fail-alloc` exists for testing but is not documented for readers. (Claude's R157 hand-back makes the same point.)

Smaller: the `E_PARIMITA` "pankti 2" is the block's line, not the failing line, and the chapter does not say so — a beginner who hits it will look at line 2 and find the `mandapa` line, which is actually the useful line, but they will not know why. And the chapter never says what `kosh ankah[2]`'s `[2]` means for growth — §1 does say "room for two to start with, holding none", which covers it.

**If something essential is missing, name it:** the kosh growth policy and what `parimana()` counts for a `kosh` (capacity, not length). Teach those two, and the chapter's own final example becomes checkable instead of magical.
