Philosophy

Sutram सूत्रम्

Sutram is a programming language whose keywords are Sanskrit. That is a design commitment, not decoration — and it holds in three layers.

Layer one

The words

yadi, yavat, prakriya are not Sanskrit translations of if, while, function — they are the language's words. A programmer writing Sutram is writing in Sanskrit's technical vocabulary, the same vocabulary Indian mathematicians and grammarians used.

The language packs extend this to ten Indian languages, but Sanskrit is the base.

Layer two

The name

सूत्रम् means sūtra — a thread, a rule. Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī, the world's first formal grammar, is a book of roughly four thousand sūtras that generate an entire language from a finite set of precise rules.

Sutram is named after that tradition of terse, exact specification. It is a deliberate ancestor.

Layer three

The methods

The standard library is not a translation of other languages' libraries. It is the Indian mathematical tradition, made executable — and that is a roadmap, not a sentiment: it answers the question "what do we build next?" without copying another language.

The roadmap

Pingala shipped

Chandahśāstra · ~2nd c. BCE

The binary enumeration of metres, the Meru-prastāra (Pascal's triangle), and the mātrā-meru (Fibonacci numbers) — in a treatise on poetry. Shipped as the chandas module.

Āryabhaṭa next

Āryabhaṭīya · ~499 CE

The kuṭṭaka, the "pulveriser", for linear indeterminate equations — the ancestor of the extended Euclidean algorithm.

Brahmagupta & Bhāskara II

628 CE · ~1150 CE

Bhāvanā and the chakravala, the cyclic method that solves Pell's equation. For N = 61 it gives x = 1766319049 — a case Europe first solved in 1657.

The Kerala school shipped

Mādhava · ~1400 CE

Infinite series for sine, cosine and π, over two centuries before Newton and Leibniz. Shipped as the madhava library module: π 3.141593, sin(0.5) 0.479426, cos(0.5) 0.877583, atan(0.5) 0.463648.

Kaṭapayādi

notation · Kerala

The letter-to-number system that hid numerals in verse.

Engineering and heritage point the same way

Sutram is self-sufficient — one hand-written NASM file, native machine code, no runtime, no toolchain required of the end user. The tradition it draws on was self-sufficient too: Pāṇini needed no foreign grammar, Mādhava needed no foreign calculus.

So the technical fundamentals are not separate from the philosophy — they are the same principle applied to machines.

Scope, honestly

We name only what is documented and verifiable. We do not claim these methods are "the origin of computing", and we do not use twentieth-century "Vedic mathematics" branding — because it would undermine the credibility of everything true above.
